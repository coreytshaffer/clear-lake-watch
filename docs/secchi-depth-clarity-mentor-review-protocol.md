# Secchi Depth / Clarity Mentor-Review Protocol

**Status:** mentor-review-needed  
**Audience:** faculty mentors, internship reviewers, technical reviewers, and future field collaborators  
**Prepared:** 2026-05-17  
**Current decision:** mentor-review-only for now  

This protocol draft defines a bounded student-led method for future Secchi depth / clarity observations in Clear Lake. It is a mentor-review packet, not an approved field protocol, QAPP, public monitoring program, or public data collection workflow.

Clear Lake Watch remains a late prototype / early MVP. It is not official public-health guidance, an official advisory, recreation guidance, emergency guidance, a validated forecast, a deployed sensor network, or an active public submission system.

## Purpose

Secchi depth is a low-cost field measurement of water transparency. It can help a student practice method discipline, site metadata, repeatability, observation-condition notes, and public wording limits before attempting more sensitive field, lab, microscopy, sensor, or cyanotoxin-related work.

This protocol is designed to answer one narrow question:

> Can a future Clear Lake Watch field note record a repeatable water-transparency observation with enough metadata, QA review, and public-boundary language to be useful for mentor feedback?

It is not designed to answer whether water is safe, whether a bloom is toxic, whether cyanobacteria are present, or whether an advisory should be issued.

| Field | Draft requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Variable | Secchi depth / water transparency |
| Unit | Meters, recorded to the nearest 0.1 m unless a mentor recommends another precision |
| Instrument | Standard Secchi disk with measured line |
| Observation type | Future field observation, private until reviewed |

## What Not To Infer

Do not use a Secchi reading by itself to claim:

- safe or unsafe recreation conditions;
- cyanotoxin risk;
- bloom severity;
- cyanobacteria density;
- current advisory status;
- official water-quality condition;
- cause of poor clarity.

Transparency can be affected by algae, suspended sediment, dissolved color, weather, glare, waves, observer differences, and site/depth conditions. The observation is a clarity measurement, not a diagnosis.

## Candidate Site Rules

Before any field observation:

- select one candidate site for mentor review;
- identify whether the site is public, private, restricted, or sensitive;
- record only a generalized public site label until location precision is reviewed;
- do not sample from unsafe, restricted, or privately controlled access points;
- do not imply the site represents the whole lake or lake arm;
- keep unresolved site-registry locations marked `needs-local-review`.

## Draft Field Metadata

Each private field record should include:

- record ID;
- date and local time;
- observer name in private notes only;
- public site label;
- private exact coordinates if collected and allowed;
- public coordinate precision decision;
- lake arm or general area;
- water depth if known;
- Secchi depth in meters;
- whether the disk hit bottom before disappearing;
- cloud cover;
- sun/glare condition;
- wind speed or qualitative wind condition;
- wave/surface condition;
- recent rain/storm note if known;
- visible surface scum or floating material note;
- observer comments;
- QA status;
- permission-to-publish decision;
- reviewer comments.

## Draft Method

This method must be reviewed before field use.

1. Confirm the site is safe and permitted for observation.
2. Confirm the Secchi disk is clean, weighted, attached securely, and paired with a measured line.
3. Confirm line markings are legible and use the selected unit.
4. Record site, date, time, observer, and weather/surface conditions before taking the reading.
5. From a stable location, lower the disk on the shaded/calm side where practical.
6. Lower the disk until it disappears from view.
7. Lower it slightly farther, then raise it slowly until it reappears.
8. Repeat the lowering/raising motion until the observer can identify a consistent disappearance/reappearance depth.
9. Record the depth in meters.
10. If the disk reaches the bottom before disappearing, record the result as greater than the measured water depth at that location, not as an ordinary Secchi depth.
11. Record any conditions that could affect interpretation, including glare, waves, rain, wind, sediment disturbance, or surface scum.
12. Mark the record as private draft until QA review is complete.

## QA/QC Draft

Minimum QA questions:

- Was the same disk and line used throughout the pilot?
- Was the line checked against a ruler or tape before use?
- Were units recorded consistently?
- Was the observation repeated enough to identify a stable disappearance/reappearance point?
- Were weather, glare, wind, waves, and recent disturbance noted?
- Was the site safe, permitted, and appropriate?
- Was public coordinate precision reviewed?
- Did a reviewer approve any public summary language?

Optional mentor-review additions:

- duplicate reading by a second observer;
- same-site repeat within a short time window;
- comparison with public CLAMP/CEDEN context when available;
- paired photo for private QA only;
- paired visual bloom observation note, kept separate from the Secchi result.

## Private-To-Public Gate

A Secchi record may become public only if:

- mentor or qualified reviewer has approved the pilot protocol;
- field observation has QA status `reviewed`;
- site access and safety were appropriate;
- exact sensitive location details are removed or generalized;
- observer personal details are removed;
- permission to publish is explicit;
- public wording says water transparency, not safety or toxin risk;
- the record is tagged as a separate future field source family;
- the public mirror validator passes after any public export.

## Public Wording Template

Use:

> A reviewed student field note recorded Secchi depth / water transparency at a generalized Clear Lake site. This observation supports method learning and local clarity context only.

Avoid:

> The water is safe.

Avoid:

> The bloom is severe.

Avoid:

> Cyanotoxins are present or absent.

Avoid:

> Clear Lake Watch has validated lake conditions.

## Mentor Review Questions

1. Is Secchi depth / clarity an appropriate first student-led field protocol for this project?
2. What would need to change before this could be considered for a private pilot field note after review?
3. Is 0.1 m precision appropriate for the intended use?
4. What site constraints, safety constraints, or permissions should be added?
5. Should the protocol require duplicate readings or a second observer?
6. Which observation conditions are mandatory versus optional?
7. Should photos be allowed for private QA, and if so, what privacy/location rules should govern them?
8. What wording would accidentally imply public-health, recreation, advisory, or forecast authority?
9. How should this protocol align with Lake County QAPP/CLAMP and CEDEN/SWAMP expectations?
